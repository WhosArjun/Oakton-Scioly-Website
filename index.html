Boyceville Satellite Invitational
December 1, 2025 · Online Satellite Format
☀️ 1st — Rocks & Minerals ☀️ 3rd — Dynamic Planet ☀️ 4th — Codebusters 🌙 3rd — Dynamic Planet

Boyceville was our season opener — a satellite invitational format where schools compete remotely and results are compiled centrally. It was a great way to shake off the rust and get a read on where everyone stood heading into the winter invitationals.

Solar kicked things off with a strong showing, claiming 1st place in Rocks & Minerals — a sign of things to come from Linus Koepcke and his event partners. The Codebusters crew of Brandon Lin, Clara Yin, and Ivy Xu also delivered a solid 4th place finish in a highly competitive field.

Lunar had a bright moment of their own, notching a 3rd place in Dynamic Planet — a strong early result for a team that was still finding its chemistry.

Overall, Boyceville gave every team a confidence boost and a benchmark. The captains were already talking strategy for Flint Hill before results were even fully announced.

🏔️
Flint Hill Invitational
December 13, 2025 · Flint Hill School, Oakton VA
☀️ 1st Overall / 32 Teams ☀️ 5× First Place Events 🌙 11th Overall

Flint Hill was a statement. Competing right in our backyard in Oakton, Virginia, Solar went 1st overall out of 32 teams — a dominant performance that sent a clear message to the rest of the region.

Solar swept five events with first-place finishes: Astronomy, Chemistry Lab, Codebusters, Machines, and Remote Sensing. Chunmin and Chunjie Huang were nearly unstoppable in the build events, while Brandon Lin and the Codebusters squad were heads above the competition.

Lunar finished 11th, with standout moments including a 1st in Dynamic Planet and 2nd in Codebusters — Jennifer Xu and the crew showed they belong on any invitational stage.

The team celebrated afterward with an unofficial "Flint Hill Pizza Party" tradition. The vibe was immaculate. This win set the tone for the entire rest of the season.

🏛️
Fairfax Invitational
January 10, 2026 · Fairfax High School · 70 Teams
☀️ 2nd Overall / 70 Teams ☀️ 8× First Place Events 🌑 3rd — Hovercraft

Fairfax was massive — 70 teams from across Virginia and the mid-Atlantic region. This was the biggest invitational of the year, and Solar showed up to compete.

Solar finished 2nd overall, racking up an eye-popping eight first-place event finishes: Astronomy, Chemistry Lab, Codebusters, Dynamic Planet, Experimental Design, Machines, Remote Sensing, and Rocks & Minerals. The consistency was remarkable across every discipline.

Eclipse had a surprise highlight, grabbing 3rd in Hovercraft — Artem Godlevskii and Holden Louie put in serious build work to pull that off in such a large field.

Missing the top spot by a razor-thin margin stung a little, but the team left Fairfax with sky-high confidence. Everyone could see that Solar was a legitimate state contender.

🦃
Virginia Tech Invitational
January 31, 2026 · Virginia Tech, Blacksburg VA
☀️ 4th Overall / 32 Teams ☀️ 3× First Place Events 🌑 2nd — Entomology 🌙 12th Overall

Virginia Tech Invitational is always one of the toughest fields of the year, with elite programs from all over the state descending on Blacksburg. Solar went 4th overall in a field packed with state-caliber competition.

Solar's brightest moments came in Codebusters (1st), Entomology (1st), and Rocks & Minerals (1st) — three dominant performances that showed the depth of the team's study event preparation. The Huang twins delivered another strong Helicopter result, and the Exp. Design squad shone with a 2nd place.

Eclipse had a remarkable moment: 2nd place in Entomology — Alexander Yu and Chunjie Huang couldn't be stopped on bug ID. Eclipse also grabbed 4th in Hovercraft.

Lunar ended at 12th overall, with a 4th place in Anatomy & Physiology leading the way. The team was building chemistry at just the right time heading into regionals.

The Blacksburg trip also doubled as a bonding experience — long van rides, late-night Cookout runs, and the shared intensity of competing at a college campus made it unforgettable.

🎓
Princeton Invitational
February 7, 2026 · Princeton University, NJ
☀️ 3rd — Dynamic Planet ☀️ 3rd — Rocks & Minerals ☀️ Top 10 in 8 Events

Princeton Invitational is one of the most prestigious invitationals in the country — hosted at Princeton University and drawing teams from up and down the East Coast. Solar made the trip to New Jersey and competed in a truly elite field.

Linus Koepcke delivered back-to-back podium finishes, claiming 3rd in both Dynamic Planet and Rocks & Minerals. The Codebusters squad landed 6th in an absolutely stacked bracket, and Ivy Xu and Clara Yin put up a 6th in Fermi Questions in their first time competing in that event.

Competing at Princeton is an experience unlike any other — the campus is beautiful, the competition is fierce, and the prestige is real. Solar held their own against some of the best programs in the country, which gave everyone a huge confidence boost heading into regionals just three weeks later.

Virginia Regional Tournament @ UMW
February 28, 2026 · University of Mary Washington, Fredericksburg VA
☀️ 6th Overall ☀️ 2× First Place ☀️ Qualified for States 🌙 3rd — Entomology

Regionals was the moment everything was building toward. Held at the University of Mary Washington in Fredericksburg, this was the official Virginia regional qualifier, and every team left it all on the floor.

Solar qualified for States with a 6th place finish, highlighted by 1st place in Codebusters (Brandon Lin, Clara Yin, and Ivy Xu were incredible under pressure) and 1st in Rocks & Minerals (Linus Koepcke and Alexander Yu continued their dominance).

Lunar had a standout moment with a 3rd place in Entomology, a fantastic result that showed how much that team had grown over the season. They finished 21st overall.

Eclipse also showed up, with a 7th in Eng. CAD and 8th in Anatomy & Physiology — strong finishes that the team was proud of.

The energy in the van on the way home was electric. Solar was headed to States. The whole team knew something special was within reach.

Virginia State Tournament @ UVA
March 21, 2026 · University of Virginia, Charlottesville VA
☀️ 2nd in Virginia ☀️ 4× First Place Events ☀️ Best Finish in Oakton History

This is the one. Virginia States at the University of Virginia — the most important tournament of the year, and the best day in Oakton Scioly history.

Solar finished 2nd in the entire state of Virginia. Let that sink in. Out of every Science Olympiad team in Virginia, Solar was second best. It is the highest placement any Oakton Scioly team has ever achieved.

The event performances were breathtaking. Solar claimed 1st place in four events: Rocks & Minerals, Codebusters, Entomology, and Water Quality. Four state champions in one tournament. Linus, Brandon, Sara, Sophia, and the whole squad were absolutely locked in.

They also podiumed in Chemistry Lab (2nd), Dynamic Planet (2nd), Machines (2nd), Remote Sensing (2nd), Experimental Design (3rd), and Materials Science (3rd) — consistent excellence across every discipline.

The atmosphere at UVA that day was something the team will never forget. Parents, friends, alumni — everyone came out. When the final results were announced and Solar's name appeared at #2, the roar was deafening.

Thank you to every captain, every member, every coach, and every parent who made this season possible. This was Oakton Scioly's year. ☀️🥈

🎯 Tryout Information

Interested in joining Oakton Science Olympiad? Here's everything you need to know about tryouts for the 2026–2027 season. We welcome students of all backgrounds : passion for science and dedication to learning are what matter most!

📅
Tryout Date: TBD — Fall 2026 Keep an eye on our Instagram @oaktonscioly and check your school email for announcements. Tryouts typically happen in September or October.
🧬
Who Can Try Out?

Any currently enrolled Oakton High School student in grades 9–12 is eligible to try out. No prior Science Olympiad experience is required — we love teaching new members!

📋
What to Expect
  • A written science knowledge test covering biology, chemistry, physics, and earth science
  • An optional build/engineering challenge component
  • Interview or brief conversation with captains
  • Results announced within 1–2 weeks
📚
How to Prepare
  • Browse the Scioly.org wiki for event overviews
  • Review current Science Olympiad events on soinc.org
  • Practice with past tests from scioly.org/tests
  • Brush up on general STEM concepts from your classes
🏗️
Build Event Interest?

If you're interested in engineering events (helicopter, hovercraft, boomilever, etc.), mention it during tryouts! Prior experience with building, robotics, or engineering is a big plus but not required.

🤝
Team Structure

Oakton Scioly has three teams: Solar ☀️, Lunar 🌙, Eclipse 🌑. Each team has 15 members and competes in invitationals and the regional tournament. Solar also competes at States.

💬
Questions?

Reach out to us on Instagram @oaktonscioly or email us at ohssciol@gmail.com. Our captains are happy to answer questions and help you prepare. Don't be shy we were all new once!

⏰
Time Commitment

Science Olympiad is a serious commitment but an incredibly rewarding one. Here's a rough breakdown of what to expect:

  • Practices: 1–2 weekly meetings after school, plus independent study with your event partner
  • Invitationals: 4–6 full-day Saturday competitions from November through February
  • Regionals: One full weekend day in late February
  • States (Solar only): One full day in March at a Virginia university
  • Build events require additional time outside of meetings to build and test devices

About Oakton Scioly

Oakton Science Olympiad is a student-led STEM competition team focused on science, engineering, inquiry, and teamwork.

🧪
Who We Are

Oakton Scioly competes in invitationals, regional tournaments, and the Virginia State Tournament every season. Our members work across biology, chemistry, physics, engineering, earth science, and coding events.

🏆
Achievements

During the 2025–2026 season, Solar placed 2nd at Virginia States, the best finish in Oakton Scioly history. Our team consistently places at top tier invitationals such as Princeton and VTech.

⚙️
Build Events

Members design and test engineering devices including helicopters, electric vehicles, hovercrafts, boomilevers, and robot systems.

📖
Study Events

Members study events from Earth Sciences (Entomology, Dynamic Planet, Water Quality) to the human body (Anatomy) and beyond (Astronomy). Members build binders and reference sheets for competition.

🌌
Team Culture

Beyond competing, Oakton Scioly is a community built on collaboration, late-night studying (on Discord), inside jokes, snacks, and teamwork. It's a second family to all of us.
